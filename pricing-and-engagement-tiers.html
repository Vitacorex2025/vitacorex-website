Pricing & engagement tiers · Published

Three tiers, published fees, no contingency.

Procurement shouldn't have to negotiate a range out of us. This page publishes the three engagement tiers, what's inside each, how billing and termination work, and where outcome-based components are permitted. Actual engagement price within each band is set during structured intake based on portfolio size, document quality, and whether the work runs inside your environment or ours. No contingency. No multi-year lock-in. No exit penalty.

Why bands, not a single price

A 3-location dental DSO with $400K in AR and a 30-location hospital network with $40M in AR need the same playbook but not the same volume of work. Bands let us disclose the honest price range without pretending one number fits both. Within-band placement is explained in writing during intake.

The three tiers

Fixed fees where we can. Bands where scope genuinely varies.

All three tiers share the same underlying methodology — structured documentation discipline, compliance-boundary-respecting recovery workflow, and file-readiness that keeps counsel engagement practical. The tier determines depth and duration, not quality of work.

ROI framework

How we measure, and what "works" actually means.

The most common procurement question is "what's the ROI." Here's our honest answer with the math we actually use.

What we count
  • Cash recovered — verifiable against operator GL, not our dashboard numbers
  • DSO reduction — days-sales-outstanding, calculated on operator's own aging report
  • Write-off avoidance — dollars that would have landed in bad-debt absent the workflow
  • Dispute resolution cycle time — days from dispute-open to closure / escalation decision
  • Escalation file-readiness score — qualitative, audited against a packet checklist
What we don't count
  • "Contact rate" as a primary KPI — it incentivizes volume over quality
  • "Promise-to-pay" without cash follow-through — promises aren't dollars
  • Recovery percentages on aged balances where solvency was the block — honest attribution, not vanity credit
  • Customer-relationship cost — we avoid tactics that improve short-term cash at the cost of lifetime value
  • Results on data we haven't seen yet — forecasts are bands, not single numbers
Standard benchmark for a 90-day Pilot: 2.2–4.4× return on pilot fees in recovered cash, measured against the operator's own GL by day 90. If the month-2 review indicates the benchmark cannot be met, we recommend ending early rather than extending. This is published because it should be — procurement deserves a number to hold us to.
