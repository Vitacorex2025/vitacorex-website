Business Plans & Market Research

An investor-readable business plan or a focused market-research memo — built to survive a due-diligence read. Positioning, unit economics, competitive landscape, and a 3-year financial model with sensitivity ranges. One plan up to 30 pages plus appendix, delivered as a PDF with editable source files in 10–14 business days.

Important: VitaCoreX LLC prepares plan documentation and market research. We do not solicit investors, issue securities, or provide investment or legal advice. Fundraising strategy, securities offerings, and valuation opinions require licensed advisors (attorneys, registered broker-dealers, or licensed appraisers). Our role is to produce the clean, evidence-backed package.

What the plan contains

Most business plans fail diligence for the same reasons: the market size is asserted rather than sourced, the financial model hides assumptions inside hard-coded cells, and the competitive analysis reads like a brochure. The VitaCoreX plan is built backwards from those failure modes. Every sizing figure is cited, every model driver is exposed as an input, and the competitive map names specific operators with defensible positioning.

Market sizing & demand signal

Top-down TAM/SAM/SOM sourced from census data, industry reports, and public filings, with a bottom-up cross-check built from addressable unit counts and realistic capture rates. We explicitly separate the total addressable market (what the category is worth globally) from the serviceable obtainable market (what your business can realistically close in years one through three). Every number carries a citation.

Competitive landscape

Named direct and adjacent competitors with a positioning map on the two axes that matter for your category (usually price vs. depth of service, or speed vs. customization). We identify white space, price-point gaps, and segment weaknesses — not generic "low competition" claims. The landscape section is what investors read first to decide whether your plan deserves the rest of their attention.

Unit economics

Customer acquisition cost, gross margin, contribution margin, payback period, and lifetime value — each derived from your actual (or modeled) cost structure rather than industry averages. For services businesses, we break out billable utilization and realization. For product businesses, we separate COGS from fulfillment. The unit economics page is typically the one investors photocopy and take to their partners.

Financial model

3-year projection

Monthly model for year one, quarterly for years two and three. Revenue built up from unit drivers (customers, ASP, retention), not top-line growth percentages. Cost structure split into fixed, variable, and step-variable (the costs that jump at capacity thresholds). Gross margin, operating margin, and EBITDA all roll up from the underlying drivers, so when an investor pushes on an assumption, the entire model updates.

Sensitivity ranges

Three scenarios — downside, base, and upside — each with a named rationale rather than arbitrary percentages. The downside case shows how much runway you have if acquisition is 40% slower than base. The upside case shows what capacity and working-capital constraints become binding first. Sensitivity tables on the two or three most-debated assumptions (typically CAC, ARPU, and churn).

Cash flow & runway

Monthly cash flow with collections timing (not just revenue recognition), working-capital requirements, and the explicit runway calculation: how many months of cash remain at each scenario's burn rate. For pre-revenue plans, the runway table shows the latest date by which the next round must close. For revenue-positive plans, the table shows the month self-sustaining operations begin.

Deliverables

Executive summary (2 pages)

A standalone 2-page document: the opportunity, the solution, the traction or validation, the ask, and the use of funds. This is what most investors actually read before deciding whether to open the full plan. Written so it survives being forwarded without the rest of the package.

Full plan (up to 30 pages + appendix)

Company overview, market analysis, competitive landscape, product or service description, go-to-market, operations, team, financial plan, risks and mitigations, and an appendix with source citations and model assumptions. Delivered as a print-ready PDF and as editable source files (Word or Google Docs, and Excel or Google Sheets) so you can update it between investor meetings.

Pitch-deck draft (10 pages)

A 10-page investor deck draft matching the plan: problem, solution, market, traction, business model, competition, team, financials, ask, and use of funds. Delivered as Google Slides or PowerPoint source so your designer can apply brand treatment. The deck is pre-aligned with the plan's numbers — no contradictions between documents.

Process

  1. Intake & scoping: 45-minute working session covering the business concept, customer segment, stage, and funding context. We identify the 2–3 assumptions the plan will live or die on, and agree on scope before any work begins.
  2. Market research: Sizing, competitive landscape, and demand-signal analysis. We share source citations as we go — no surprises in the final document.
  3. Financial model build: Draft model reviewed with you in a second working session. You see every driver and can push back on any assumption before it hardens into the plan narrative.
  4. Plan draft: Full plan and executive summary drafted against the finalized model. Shared as a commented Google Doc or Word file for your red-line.
  5. Final delivery: Revision pass on your comments, pitch deck drafted against the plan, and final package delivered as PDF + editable source files. One clarification pass included within 30 days of delivery.
