Section 1

Executive summary.

Written for the CFO and COO audience — five observations, each tied to a dollar magnitude the finance team can recognize. No recommendation yet; recommendations sit in Section 5 after the findings are established.

  • Identified recoverable AR across the network totals $[REDACTED]M — concentrated in balances aged 60–180 days with mixed packet quality.

Section 2

Methodology.

The 30 days broke down into three phases: data collection (days 1–10), sampling and audit (days 11–22), synthesis and executive review (days 23–30). Nothing in this methodology requires system access beyond read-only views the operator controls.

  • Aging report pull from each site — weekly snapshots across the trailing 90 days, reconciled to the operator's own financial close.
  • Statistically sampled packet quality review — 120 balances drawn across sites, aging buckets, and service types. Each packet scored against a 12-item rubric.
  • Escalation threshold audit — one interview per site with the billing manager, focused on observed practice rather than written policy.
  • Patient-path walkthrough — shadow exercise of the current patient experience from statement receipt to portal login, on each site's production configuration.
  • Contract and payer-mix review — counsel-facing, scope limited to reading existing agreements and flagging ambiguity. No contract renegotiation within diagnostic scope.
Section 3

Findings, by domain.

Each finding follows the same four-line format: Observation → Impact → Root cause → Recommendation. This is the same template the live deliverable uses so every finding can be evaluated independently by the operator's team.

Finding 3.1 — Documentation timing

Observation: [REDACTED]% of 90+-day balances lack an itemized statement matched to the service date. Impact: every patient or payer challenge requires a back-office research cycle averaging [REDACTED] days before a response can be sent. Root cause: documentation is generated at billing, not re-generated at escalation — by day 90 it is often stale. Recommendation: regenerate a fresh packet at each escalation threshold, not at initial billing.

Finding 3.2 — Escalation variance

Observation: across [REDACTED] sites, escalation to outside action occurs between day 60 and day 130 depending on billing manager preference. Impact: two patients with identical balance profiles experience materially different timelines, creating fairness and compliance exposure. Root cause: no written network-wide escalation policy; thresholds inherited from pre-acquisition practice. Recommendation: unified thresholds at 45 / 75 / 120 days with documented override criteria.

Finding 3.3 — Payment-path inconsistency

Observation: [REDACTED] of [REDACTED] sites send paper-only statements with no digital fallback. Impact: patients who prefer digital channels receive friction at the exact moment the network needs cooperation. Root cause: portal rollout paused during [REDACTED] transition; paper pathway never retired. Recommendation: extend portal option to all sites with paper retained as fallback — no site forced digital-only.

Finding 3.4 — EHR-to-practice reconciliation gap

Observation: [REDACTED] of [REDACTED] sites show an unreconciled balance delta between the EHR and the practice-management system, ranging $[REDACTED]K to $[REDACTED]K per site. Impact: finance reporting understates true AR by an aggregated $[REDACTED]K across the network. Root cause: reconciliation cadence was weekly at acquisition, became monthly post-acquisition, then lapsed entirely at three sites. Recommendation: weekly reconciliation restored network-wide with variance tolerance published.

Finding 3.5 — Counsel-handoff readiness

Observation: when balances reach the point of outside enforcement, the file packet readiness averages [REDACTED]% against the 90% threshold counsel requires to act without further research. Impact: counsel either delays action or charges additional preparation hours — both slow recovery and erode margin. Root cause: no single template for what the handoff packet must contain. Recommendation: codify a handoff template and measure file readiness as a standing KPI.

No payer renegotiations. No new financing facility. Operator retains all patient-contact execution. No outside counsel engaged during the six-month window unless the engagement crosses the 120-day threshold. Every assumption failure narrows the band toward the low end.

Section 5

90-day prioritized roadmap.

This is the sequencing the diagnostic recommends. It is not a quote — scope and price are negotiated separately. Published here so the operator's team can evaluate whether the sequencing fits their operational capacity before discussing commercial terms.

Days 1–30

Unified aging view, pilot sites, packet template

Build cross-site aging dashboard. Select [REDACTED] pilot sites covering volume and specialty mix. Publish the patient-balance packet template. No network-wide rollout in this window — containment until the pilot validates.

Days 31–60

Days 61–90

Set thresholds at 45 / 75 / 120 days. Publish counsel-handoff packet template. Begin handback of reporting cadence to operator's team with VCX in observer role for the final two weeks of the 90-day window.

Section 6

Out of scope for this diagnostic.

The diagnostic scope is deliberately narrow. Anything listed here is either counsel-side, operator-side, or a separate commercial engagement.

  • No legal representation or legal advice. Counsel-side work remains with the operator's own counsel; this diagnostic flags items for counsel, it does not resolve them.
  • No debt-collection activity. No balance is contacted, assigned, or referred by VitaCoreX inside the diagnostic window; patient contact remains the operator's role.
  • No HIPAA policy design. Compliance artifacts (NPP, BAA templates, breach-response policies) remain the operator's internal responsibility.
  • No payer contract renegotiation. Contracts are read for ambiguity only; any renegotiation is a separate engagement under a separate SOW.
  • No PHI processing on VitaCoreX-owned infrastructure. All workflow tooling runs inside the operator's controlled environment during the diagnostic window.
  • No warranty of specific recovery outcome. The recovery band in Section 4 is a modeled range, not a guarantee — contract terms reflect this explicitly.
